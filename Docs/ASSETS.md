# Editing art and rooms

Everything in `assets/` can be edited and rebuilt with `.\build.ps1`; the Makefile notices changed files and
runs the converter (`scripts/assets.py`). If something breaks a rule below, the build stops with a message
naming the file, the frame, metatile or object, and the position.

Placeholder art was generated by one-off scripts (Phase 1 and 2, 2026-10-17). From now on **the PNGs and
Tiled files are the source**; edit them directly.

After changing rooms, re-record the test run (`.\scripts\record-run.ps1`, DEBUGGING.md): the recorded
button presses only fit the rooms they were recorded in.

## Every graphic exists twice

The owner chose separate art per console (2026-10-17): each graphic is a pair with the **same layout**:

| File | Used by | Colours |
|---|---|---|
| `<name>.gbc.png` | `doors.gbc` | Any RGB colours, within the GBC limits below |
| `<name>.dmg.png` | `doors.gb` | Exactly the 4 DMG greys: `#FFFFFF`, `#AAAAAA`, `#555555`, `#000000` |

Sprites use transparency (alpha) for "no pixel"; backgrounds have none.

Tiled shows the `.gbc.png`. After changing it, make the matching change in the `.dmg.png` (or the DMG ROM
shows the old picture there). The two don't have to use "the same" shades: the DMG wallpaper, for example,
is drawn a shade darker than a straight conversion so the wall stays dark.

## Tilesets (`assets/tilesets/`)

A tileset is a grid of **16×16 metatiles** (one DOORS "block"): `hotel.gbc.png`, `hotel.dmg.png` and
`hotel.tsx` (the Tiled tileset). The image width must stay a multiple of 16; the `.tsx` says how many columns.

Rules:

- **GBC:** at most **4 colours in each 8×8 quarter** of a metatile, and at most **7 different 4-colour
  palettes** across the whole tileset (palette 8 is the font's). Colours are compared at GBC precision
  (5 bits per channel), so two colours that differ only slightly count as one.
- **DMG:** only the 4 greys.
- At most **184** different 8×8 tiles per console after duplicates are removed (the hotel set uses 90) and
  **120** metatiles. The rest of the background's VRAM holds door-number plates and the font.

Metatile properties, set in Tiled (select the tile in the tileset, Properties panel):

| Property | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | string | Becomes a constant in code, e.g. `MT_HOTEL_DOOR_TOP`. **The game uses the door, dresser and doorway names below by name**, so don't rename those |
| `solid` | bool | The player can't walk into it |
| `bookcase` | bool | A bookcase's bottom half: one of the Library's books can be here if there's floor below it |

Metatile 0 must stay the **void** (solid black). It's used outside the room and for empty cells.

The hotel metatiles:

| Metatiles | Notes |
|---|---|
| void, floor, floor_shadow, carpet_l/m/r | `floor_shadow` is the floor row below a wall face |
| wall_top, wallpaper, wallpaper_shadow, wainscot | The wallpaper is the Minecraft version's wall: `green_terracotta.png` from `F:\Minecraft DOORS Project\_ResourcePack\assets\minecraft\textures\block\` (owner's request, 2026-10-17), a lighter olive diamond on darker olive |
| door_top, door_bottom, door_bottom_locked, door_open_top, door_open_bottom | A door in the top wall: 1 wide, 2 tall (wallpaper row + wainscot row). Layout (owner, 2026-10-18): a dark header (rows 0–8 of door_top) with the number, then a whole door (frame, panel, knob at rows 20–21 of the door's 32). **The number area (x 2–12, y 2–6 of the top half) is painted over by the game**: brass digits (the palette's lightest colour) on the dark (its darkest); the art shows "000" there. It must stay within the top 8 pixel rows. door_open_top keeps the header (the number shows over an open doorway) |
| side_door_top, side_door_bottom, side_door_bottom_locked, side_open_top, side_open_bottom | A door in the left or right wall, seen from above (symmetric, so one set serves both walls). The shut ones are drawn like the top-wall door; side_open_* is floor |
| doorway_s | An open doorway in the bottom wall (entries only) |
| closet_top, closet_bottom, dresser, dresser_open, table, desk_l/m/r | Furniture. dresser_open is a dresser after its drawer was searched |
| bed_top, bed_bottom | A bed, headboard and pillow above the blanket (Phase 3; metatiles 29 and 30) |
| shelf_top_a/b/c, shelf_bottom_a/b/c | The Library's bookcases (Phase 6), 1×2: a top above a bottom, three mixes of spine colours each (two new palettes: red and cream, green and blue; the door palette's brown and gold). The bottoms are `bookcase`. All solid |
| desk_paper | The desk's middle with the paper on it (the Library's `paper` object sits on it) |
| door_bottom_padlock | A top-wall door's bottom half with the combination padlock (the Library's exit while it's locked; the game swaps it in) |

## Rooms (`assets/rooms/`)

A room is a Tiled map (`.tmx`) using one tileset:

- One tile layer, saved as **CSV** (Map → Map Properties → Tile Layer Format). Don't flip or rotate tiles.
- Size: up to 127 metatiles per side and 2,816 in total (WRAM limit, `ROOM_MAX` in `src/room.h`; 3,072 until
  Phase 6 needed the WRAM).
- **Map properties** (Map → Map Properties → Custom Properties):

  | Property | Type | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | `kind` | string | `normal` (the generator uses it), `start` (the reception, room 0; exactly one), `test` (never used in a run), `halt` (Halt's hallway, loaded by `halt.c`; at most one; entry on the left wall, exit on the right) or `library` (always room 50; at most one; below) |
  | `allow_lock` | bool | The room may lock its exit (18% chance) and hide the key inside it. Needs at least one drawer or key spot |

- **Objects** on an object layer, named:

  | Name | Shape | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | `entry` | rectangle on the door, property `wall` = bottom, left or right | The door you come in by. Exactly one in a normal room |
  | `exit` | rectangle on the door, property `wall` = top, left or right | The door to the next room. One, or 2–3 for a **Dupe room**: the run picks the real one, the others are wrong doors (Dupe's, or numberless fakes) |
  | `drawer` | 1×1 rectangle on a `dresser` metatile | Can be searched with A; may hold the key. At most 8 per room |
  | `keyspot` | point (or 1×1 rectangle) | A place a key can lie in the open. Property `fixed` = true marks the start room's key (always there) |
  | `spawn` | point | Start and test rooms: where the player starts (top left of the 16×16 sprite) |
  | `closet` | 1×2 rectangle on `closet_top` + `closet_bottom` | A hiding spot (A to get in, A or B to get out) |
  | `bed` | 1×2 rectangle on `bed_top` + `bed_bottom` | A hiding spot, under the bed |
  | `paper` | 1×1 rectangle on `desk_paper` | The Library: the paper (A reads it) |
  | `figure` | point | The Library: where Figure starts |

  Rectangles must line up with the 16×16 grid (hold Ctrl while drawing in Tiled). Door sizes: top wall 1×2
  (rows 1–2), left/right wall 1×2 in the first/last column, bottom wall 1×1 in the last row. Put the matching
  closed-door metatiles there; the game swaps in open/closed/locked versions. At most 8 closets and beds
  together per room.

- **Hiding spots matter for Rush and Ambush**: a spawn waits for a room with a closet or bed (Hide's bans
  aside), so a room with none is a breather. Every normal template has at least one so far. Leave the cell
  in front of (or beside) each one walkable.
- **Rush's route** through a room is the straight line between the centres of its entry and exit doors
  (reception: spawn to exit; a Dupe room: to the run's real exit, one route per exit). Nothing to draw: the
  converter works it out. He passes through furniture and
  walls on the way, as in the Minecraft version.

- **Which way rooms connect** (owner, 2026-10-17): exits go up or sideways, never down. A room entered from
  the bottom follows a room that left through the top; entered from the left follows one that left through
  the right, and so on. The converter checks that every exit wall has at least one room that can follow it.

**3/4 view convention**: the top edge of a wall is `wall_top`. A wall the player faces from below shows its
face under it: `wallpaper`, then `wainscot`, then a row of `floor_shadow`. Side walls and the bottom wall are
only `wall_top`. Furniture is 1 metatile (dresser, table) or 2 tall (closet: `closet_top` above
`closet_bottom`; bed: `bed_top` above `bed_bottom`). Leave the cell in front of each door and drawer walkable: that's where the player stands
to use it.

Current templates: `reception` (start), `hall_ns`, `bedroom`, `lounge` (bottom → top), `turn_left`,
`turn_right` (bottom → side), `left_to_top`, `right_to_top` (side → top), `hall_we`, `hall_ew` (side →
side), and `test` (the Phase 1 scrolling test room). Hiding spots (Phase 3): two closets in the reception
and the lounge, two closets and two beds in the bedroom, one closet in every other template.

Phase 5 (2026-10-18): Dupe rooms `dupe_two_r` and `dupe_two_l` (bottom → top and right / left),
`dupe_three` (bottom → top, left and right; two beds), `dupe_side_l` and `dupe_side_r` (side → top and the
far side), each with a closet or beds, two drawers and a key spot; and `halt` (kind `halt`): a 38×8 hallway,
36 metatiles of empty floor between a left door and a right one. A Dupe room's doors are on different walls,
so each has its own plate (exits use plate slots 0–2 in object order).

Phase 6: `library` (kind `library`, 30×27): entry at the bottom middle, the padlocked exit at the top
middle, six rows of bookcases either side of a carpet (120 bookcase bottoms with an aisle below: where books
can be), the desk with the paper, three closets, Figure on the carpet. The converter checks a library has
one bottom entry, one top exit, a `paper` and a `figure`, 8–255 places for books, a solid edge (Figure's
route search relies on it), and at most 1,152 metatiles (`(ROOM_MAX - 512) / 2`: the search uses the rest
of the room map). It also checks that every wall a room can be entered by has a normal room with only a
top door, for room 49.

To preview a room as an image: `F:\Programs\Tiled\tmxrasterizer.exe --scale 2 assets\rooms\lounge.tmx build\preview\lounge.png`.

## Sprites (`assets/sprites/`)

A sheet is `<name>.gbc.png`, `<name>.dmg.png` and `<name>.json`:

```json
{ "frame_width": 16, "frame_height": 16, "frames": ["down_idle", "down_a", ...] }
```

Frames are laid out in a grid left to right, top to bottom, in the order of `frames`. Frame names become
constants (`SPR_PLAYER_DOWN_IDLE`). A 16×16 frame is drawn as two 8×16 hardware sprites, the left and right
half; an empty half costs nothing. Bigger frames (multiples of 16, e.g. 32×32) are cut into 16×16 parts, and
the constant is the first part's number.

Rules:

- **GBC:** at most **3 colours** (plus transparent) in each 8×16 half, and at most 8 palettes per sheet.
- **DMG:** each 8×16 half uses either white or light grey, not both (plus dark grey and black). The hardware
  has two sprite palettes; the converter picks one per half.

Sheets: `player` (`down_idle`, `down_a`, `down_b`, `up_idle`, `up_a`, `up_b`, `left_idle`, `left_a`,
`left_b`; right-facing is the left frames mirrored; walk cycle idle → a → idle → b), `key` (one frame), and
`rush` and `ambush` (32×32, frames `f0` and `f1`, alternated every 4 frames: the second is the first with
its smoky edge jittered). Phase 5 (owner's picks from mock-ups, 2026-10-18): `halt` (32×32 frames, the
24×32 ghost in the left part so the right column is empty; `f0`/`f1`, the hem rippling) reduced from the
pack's `halt.png` to navy, cyan and white, the eyes painted back in (they vanish in a straight reduction);
`timothy` (an 8×8 spider in the top left of a 16×16 frame) from the pack's `timothypet.png`, legs drawn
back in; `letters` (16×16 frames `tu`, `rn`, `ar`, `ou`, `nd`: TURN AROUND as 8×16 letters, white with a
navy outline). They were made by a script from the pack textures; to change them, edit the PNGs. Phase 6 (owner's picks from mock-ups): `figure` (32×48 frames
`f0`/`f1`, the left leg lifted in the second), drawn by hand after the DOORS wiki render the owner pointed
to (`static.wikitide.net/doorswiki/thumb/6/67/Figure_render.png/939px-Figure_render.png`): red-orange body,
navy outline, a pale ring of teeth for a head and his hand over his ribs; `marks` (16×16 frames, the art in
the top-left 8×8: `glint`, the book's sparkle; `arrow_l`, `arrow_u`, `arrow_d`, the edge arrow; right is
`arrow_l` mirrored). Rush and
Ambush were pixelated (2026-10-17) from the DOORS wiki pictures the owner
pointed to (`static.wikitide.net/doorswiki/3/36/Rushvnew.webp`, `.../1/1a/Ambush_sprite.png`): Rush black
smoke with a grey face and white teeth, Ambush green and white. `eyes` (32×32, `f0`/`f1`, the aura jittered)
was reduced from the Minecraft resource pack's `assets/doors/textures/item/eyes.png` (owner's choice,
2026-10-18): white eyes edged in magenta on a dark purple aura. `screech` (16×16, `f0`/`f1`, the tentacles
moved) is drawn by hand after the pack's `screech.png`, because a straight reduction was a black blob: black
body (invisible in the dark, as it should be), white eyes, a white-and-pink grin. The art is LSPLASH's.

## Font (`assets/fonts/`)

`font.gbc.png`, `font.dmg.png`: 8×8 characters in a grid, 8 per row, in the order of `chars` in
`font.json`. One palette for the whole font (light text on black). Lower case is shown as upper case; any
character not in the font shows as a space. Special characters: `=` the HUD's top border, `~` the key icon,
`@` the heart, `*` `+` `{` `}` `#` the health bar's tiles (empty, 2, 4, 6, 8 px filled), `>` the menu cursor,
`_` the HUD's top border (it was `=` until Phase 6), `=` an equals sign, and the Library's shapes: `%` circle,
`[` square, `&` heart, `$` star, `(` X, `)` cross, `^` triangle (also the padlock's cursor), `]` diamond.
At most 64 characters (63 used).

## Title picture (`assets/title/`)

`logo.gbc.png`, `logo.dmg.png`: the DOORS logo, 144×48 (multiples of 8), shown on the title screen. GBC: 4
colours per 8×8 tile, up to 7 palettes; at most 184 different tiles. It was made (2026-10-17) by reducing the
official logo the owner pointed to (`upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2f/DOORS_Logo.webp`) to 4
colours with a 1 px outline. The logo is LSPLASH's; this is a fan project.

## Jumpscares (`assets/scares/`)

`scare_rush`, `scare_ambush`, `scare_screech`, `scare_dupe` and `scare_figure` (`.gbc.png`, `.dmg.png`): full screen, 160×144, drawn at 2×
(each art pixel is 2×2) in 4 shades, from the same pictures as the sprites. Same rules as the title picture:
4 colours per 8×8 tile on GBC and at most 184 different tiles (Rush uses 115, Ambush 171, Dupe 94 (his red
grin and eyes on black, from the pack's `dupe.png`), Figure 178 (his round head and a ring of uneven pale teeth round a dark throat, his hand over his ribs below;
drawn from the owner's reference frame, `s6.ezgif.com/tmp/ezgif-68ca71228858ff9d-gif-png/frame_12_delay-0.07s.png`), Screech 155: his
first version had 188, from speckle in the dark outline, and was simplified), since they're loaded over the
tileset while shown.

## Music (`assets/music/`)

hUGETracker songs (`.uge`, format 6, hUGETracker 1.0.11 in `tools/hugetracker`): open them there, edit, save,
rebuild. The build converts each with `uge2source` into `music_<name>`. Songs must not use timer-based tempo
(the game calls the driver once per frame).

| Song | Plays | Made from |
|---|---|---|
| `title.uge` | Title screen | The owner's `midi/Lobby Theme (Title Screen).mid`, arranged by hand: CH1 the Scifi lead, CH2 the jazz guitar stabs (and before they start, the cello chords) as arpeggios, CH3 the cello's bass notes, CH4 the drums. 72 bpm: rows alternate 13 and 12 ticks (F0D/F0C on CH4) |
| `library.uge` | The Library (room 50), from entering until you walk out | The owner's `midi/Unhinged (Library Theme).mid`: CH1 the muted guitar's ostinato, CH2 the strings' upper three voices as an arpeggio, CH3 the strings' bass. CH4 is left free for Figure's footsteps. 100 bpm = 9 ticks a row |
| `guiding_light.uge` | The death screen | `midi/Guiding Light (Death Theme).mid`: CH1 the vibraphone melody, CH2 the piano's right-hand arpeggio, CH3 the piano bass. The piano's chord stabs are left out. 75 bpm = 12 ticks a row |

In both a row is a 16th note and a pattern is 4 bars. The rooms have no music: the hotel ambience
(`hotel.uge`) was removed at the owner's request (2026-10-18). Sound effects borrow CH1, CH2 and CH4 (CH4 for
the whole of a Rush or Ambush pass), so any song that plays during a run must leave them light. The other MIDI files (Elevator
Jam, Jeff's Shop Jingle, Here I Come, Ending Theme, Unhinged II for Room 100) are for later phases and get arranged when those come.

Sound effects aren't files: they're register scripts in `src/sfx_data.c` (`s_door`, `s_flicker`, …), one table
of steps each.
